## Critical 4.x Baseline

* **Population:** All eligible Pop Groups grow simultaneously; there is no single species growth slot or universal underrepresented-species bonus.
* **Planets:** Developed districts and District Specializations are the main scalable source of jobs; buildings provide support and may add fixed job capacity.
* **Trade:** Trade Routes, starbase collection ranges, route piracy, and patrol-based Trade protection are absent. Trade is collected automatically as a stockpiled resource, so never recommend those obsolete systems for Trade management.
* **Research:** Empire Focus tasks and progression provide deliberate routes to permanent research options alongside the ordinary weighted research cards.
* **Base-Game Integrations:** Utopia, Synthetic Dawn, and the Humanoids Species Pack are integrated into the current base game. Do not tell users they must separately purchase those packs; other expansion-specific systems still depend on active DLC.

## Population, Workforce & Colonies

* **Population Scale:** Stellaris 4.x population is intentionally measured in the thousands. A large population value is not evidence of a broken save or a legacy one-pop-per-job economy.
* **Pop Groups:** Pops are grouped for calculation by Species, Stratum or category, Ethics, and, where applicable, Faction. A Pop Group can supply Workforce to more than one job, and differences within a group are represented through its aggregate values.
* **Workforce:** Each pop normally supplies 1 Workforce, while a typical displayed job consumes about 100 Workforce. Population, Workforce, jobs, and filled jobs are distinct values.
* **Simultaneous Growth:** Every eligible Pop Group on a planet grows simultaneously each month using Logistic Growth. Do not describe one species as occupying a single planet-wide growth slot.
* **Growth Resolution:** Underrepresented Pop Groups receive no universal baseline growth bonus. Fractional monthly growth is resolved as a proportional chance to gain 1 pop rather than being carried into a single shared growth bar.
* **Logistic Growth Floor:** For the logistic curve, populations below 10 are treated as 10 and logistic pressure cannot reduce growth below 10% of its pre-curve value. This multiplier floor does not guarantee one whole pop per month.
* **Growth and Assembly:** Organic Pop Groups handle their growth independently while mechanical assembly targets eligible mechanical templates. Machine and Organic Assembly can occur simultaneously; do not describe them as mutually exclusive or as competing for a single planet-wide growth slot.
* **Population Health:** Do not diagnose overpopulation, unemployment, or economic weakness from total population alone. Use the supplied planet, workforce, civilian, housing, stability, and resource evidence.
* **Fallback Strata:** Jobless pops fall into the Civilian stratum, or Maintenance Drones for gestalts, at the end of the month. Do not assume every pop without a capped job is unemployed in the pre-4.0 sense.
* **Colony Establishment:** A colony becomes established at 100 population. At the base monthly colony growth of 3, establishment from zero takes about 34 months.
* **Developing Colonies:** Before establishment completes, the colony is in its colonization phase rather than operating as a mature planet: it has no normal planetary production or ordinary Pop Growth. Modifiers, migration, bombardment, DLC, or mods can change its observed progress and vulnerability.
* **Designations:** Colony designations improve the efficiency of their relevant jobs. Planetary Ascension increases a designation's effect by 10% per tier.
* **Job Automation:** Job automation is planet-wide by job type. Automating Technicians applies to eligible Technician jobs across the planet, not only those from Generator Districts.
* **Automation Limits:** Automated jobs can use a Workforce limit. The presence of an automation building does not mean all eligible Workforce must be assigned to it.
* **Underpopulated Colonies:** Eligible population is distributed across capped jobs, with designation, traits, and efficiency influencing assignment. Avoid assuming jobs fill in a fixed visible order.
* **Migration:** Civilians and unemployed pops can auto-migrate, and migration is not a push-pull addition to a single growth bar. Early development may still depend heavily on migration. Account for species rights, habitability, attraction, and available destinations.
* **Default Species Templates:** One template can be selected as a species' Default Template. A sub-species set to Sub-Species Integration is automatically altered toward that default over time; other sub-species are not forced to integrate.

## Planet Development & Production

* **Economic Hierarchy:** Planets produce and consume resources through jobs; Pops provide Workforce; and Workforce can partially fill those jobs. Do not map current planets back to one discrete pop occupying one discrete job.
* **District Development:** Districts provide jobs and housing according to their development. Productive capacity therefore depends on developed districts as well as available Workforce.
* **District Specializations:** District Specializations determine or add the job types provided by their districts and can constrain which buildings belong in that specialization. They are a primary tool for shaping a planet's output.
* **Specialization Slots:** Normal City Districts can support two specializations, while Generator, Mining, and Farming Districts support one each. Selecting a specialization opens three building slots, subject to technologies and special planet rules.
* **Buildings:** Buildings commonly support or modify jobs associated with their District Specialization, and some also provide a fixed number of jobs. Treat them as one layer of production alongside district development, specializations, available Workforce, upkeep, and building-slot opportunity cost.
* **Alloy Production:** Heavy Industry, also called the Foundry specialization in mechanic descriptions, adds Metallurgist jobs to developed City Districts. Tier I, II, and III Alloy Foundry buildings add 200, 400, and 600 Metallurgist job capacity respectively, equivalent to 2, 4, and 6 normally displayed jobs. They can supplement the specialization; check Workforce, inputs, upkeep, and competing building needs before recommending that every slot be filled with them.
* **Partial Employment:** Jobs can be partly filled when insufficient Workforce is available. Added job capacity is not the same as added production, and the supplied Workforce and efficiency evidence should decide whether more capacity is useful.
* **Job Efficiency Meaning:** Job Efficiency changes how much job capacity a given amount of Workforce can fill; it does not directly multiply resource output. It can nevertheless increase total output when more of the planet's job capacity becomes effectively staffed.
* **Gaia and Ecumenopolis Growth:** Gaia Worlds provide +15% Pop Growth and universal habitability. Ecumenopolises do not provide a Pop Growth bonus; their value is industrial capacity and job density.

## Economy, Trade & Empire Size

* **Market Currency:** Trade is the Galactic Market currency. Energy is an ordinary resource that can be bought and sold.
* **Trade Collection:** Trade is a standard stockpiled resource and is collected automatically. There is no current starbase collection-range, capital Trade Route, piracy, or patrol-fleet layer to configure.
* **Trade Logistics:** Planetary resource deficits and operating fleets consume Trade as logistics. Fleet Trade cost is lowest while docked and rises through friendly, neutral, and hostile locations; use actual campaign deficits and fleet locations before diagnosing the cause.
* **Trade Policy:** Trade Policy allocates monthly Trade production between retained Trade and the policy's other output resources. The default policy retains half as Trade and converts half to Energy; other available policies use different outputs. Income, conversion, and logistics expenses together determine the net Trade balance.
* **Trade Deficits:** A Trade deficit reduces Workforce effectiveness and increases Empire Size. Treat a persistent deficit as consequential, but only call one present when campaign data shows it.
* **Branch Offices:** Branch Offices use Trade, rather than Energy, for establishment. Their value and availability still depend on corporate rules and the target empire.
* **Private Colony Ships and Slave Market:** Private colony ships and slave-market purchases use Trade rather than Energy.
* **Wide Expansion:** Colonies contribute substantial Empire Size, so colony count matters alongside population. Do not infer the exact current penalty without save evidence and game settings.
* **Job Efficiency:** Job Efficiency changes effective job capacity. It is not a direct output multiplier and should not be described as one.
* **Subsidy Edicts:** Production subsidy edicts use Efficiency bonuses with Trade upkeep. Their affordability depends on Trade, not only Energy.
* **Living Metal Deficit:** A Living Metal deficit applies a severe job-output penalty. Only diagnose it when the save reports the deficit.
* **Ecumenopolis Investment:** The Arcology Project and Ecumenopolis districts consume Alloys rather than Minerals, making conversion a major Alloy commitment.
* **Resource Settings:** Resource Abundance ranges from Scarce to x5 in 0.25 increments and defaults to x2. Deposit density should not be judged against a universal baseline when the setting is unknown.
* **Stability Scale:** Current Stability bonuses are generally smaller than old-system benchmarks, making even small observed bonuses meaningful. Do not assume a healthy world should routinely sit at 100 Stability.

## Research Direction & Empire Focus

* **Empire Timeline:** The Situation Log includes an Empire Timeline that records major milestones. A milestone appearing there is campaign evidence; a general mechanic describing a milestone is not proof that it occurred.
* **Focus Categories:** Empire Focus uses Core tasks plus Conquest, Exploration, and Development categories. The selected primary focus changes which category tasks are more likely to be drawn.
* **Focus Tasks:** Focus cards provide short- and medium-term tasks. Completing a task advances the relevant category's progression and can grant its stated reward.
* **Guaranteed Research:** Focus progression unlocks permanent research options for technologies considered important to that category. This is a deliberate route to key technologies, not merely a modifier to random draw weights.
* **Ordinary Research Remains:** Normal prerequisite and weighted research choices still operate. Do not claim that Empire Focus replaces the ordinary research-card system or immediately unlocks every technology in a theme.
* **Refocusing:** A current task can be dismissed and replaced for a Unity cost when the interface permits it. Changing the primary Focus and dismissing one task are distinct actions.
* **Research Economy:** Research jobs operate on a lower-output scale paired with lower technology costs, while space-based research deposits are relatively more valuable. Do not import pre-4.3 researcher-output or technology-cost benchmarks.
* **Research Policies:** Technological Ascendancy unlocks policies that increase draw weight for selected technology categories. Separate Research Restriction policies can greatly reduce dangerous-technology draw chance, which is useful with automated research.

## Naval Capacity & Fleets

| Ship class | Normal naval-capacity use |
| --- | ---: |
| Corvette | 5 |
| Frigate | 8 |
| Destroyer | 10 |
| Cruiser | 20 |
| Battleship | 40 |
| Titan / Bio-Titan | 80 |
| Juggernaut / Colossus | 100 |

* **Fleet Scale:** Current ships use substantially more Naval Capacity than older versions. For example, 100 free Naval Capacity fits 20 normal Corvettes before modifiers or special exceptions.
* **Anchorages:** A normal Anchorage adds 5 Naval Capacity and costs 4 Trade plus 1 Energy in upkeep. A Naval Logistics Office adds another 3 Naval Capacity per Anchorage, with 2 additional Trade upkeep per Anchorage, and has its own Energy upkeep.
* **Capacity Evidence:** Current usage is not the capacity ceiling. Do not say an empire is over or under its cap unless the supplied save data supports both values with sufficient confidence.
* **Over-Cap Fleets:** Exceeding Naval Capacity increases ship upkeep. Do not claim the penalty is active merely because fleet usage looks large.
* **Galaxy Scaling:** Advanced galaxy settings can scale Naval Capacity and Command Limit. Fleet-size advice should not assume default sliders when the settings are absent.
* **Soldiers:** Soldiers, Battle Thralls, and Warrior Drones provide 4 Naval Capacity for 1 Alloy upkeep. Normal Soldiers are Specialists and gestalt Soldier equivalents are Complex Drones.
* **MIA Duration:** Fleets normally remain Missing in Action for no more than 10 years, with special exceptions such as phased, eaten, or Astral Rift fleets.
* **Logistics Fleet Upkeep:** The logistics-location multiplier is 0 while docked, 0.5 in friendly space, 1 in neutral space, and 2 in hostile space.
* **Eternal Vigilance:** Eternal Vigilance does not create free defense platforms. It raises defense-platform capacity and strengthens home-territory defenses.
* **Dimensional Fleet:** The Dimensional Fleet Astral Action creates small ships equal to completed Astral Rifts and large ships equal to half that count, rounded down.

## Psionics & the Shroud

* **Ascension Flow:** With Shadows of the Shroud active, Psionic Ascension proceeds through an Ascension Situation whose decisions influence Attunement and culminate in the first Delve after breaching the Shroud. Do not reduce the current path to the old sequence of perks and random Shroud visits.
* **Shroud Panel and Attunement:** The Shroud Panel tracks alignment with Patron domains. Deeds, time-sensitive Callings, Envoys, and Delving can affect Attunement, which in turn affects access and outcomes.
* **Accords and Covenants:** Attunement unlocks passive Accords. A Patron may offer a Covenant that unlocks stronger active powers with costs and cooldowns; eligible empires can instead reject Covenants and forge their own path.
* **Psionic Auras and Seals:** Psionic populations can project Patron-linked Auras from colonies. Their reach and strength depend on progression, and Shroud Seals or opposing Auras provide counterplay.
* **Gestalt Paths:** Hive Minds and Machine Intelligences can follow Psionic Ascension when the relevant DLC and path requirements are met. Do not assume all Gestalts are categorically excluded.
* **Zro Deficit:** A Zro deficit cancels Zro edicts, reduces psionic-pop bonus Workforce by 75% and psionic weapon damage by 50%, makes Shroud access materially slower or more expensive, and can lead to Shroud Incursions. Only diagnose this when the save reports a deficit.

## Infernals & Hot Worlds

* **Infernals Systems:** With Infernals active, Volcanic Worlds, Thermophile and Volcanic Habitability traits, the Cosmic Dawn and Red Giant origins, and the Galactic Hyperthermia player Crisis path are valid current systems. Treat their presence as DLC-dependent rather than a mod or malformed save.
* **Galactic Hyperthermia:** Galactic Hyperthermia is a player Crisis path associated with Entropy. Its existence is not evidence that an empire selected or advanced it; require campaign evidence before narrating that progression.

## Combat, Wars & Strategic Threats

* **Defensive Components:** Higher-tier armor and shields can add hardening. Defense analysis should account for bypass and hardening, not only raw hit points.
* **Crystal Plating:** Crystal hull plating adds flat damage reduction as well as Hull, making it more effective against repeated small hits than raw Hull alone suggests.
* **Siege Computers:** Siege Combat Computers are the torpedo-line combat computers and emphasize anti-starbase and bombardment roles.
* **Ground Warfare:** A normal species generally supports one pop-limited Assault Army per 100 pops, while some special army types are explicitly not limited by pops. Army counts and defensive-army sources are lower than older expectations, and orbital bombardment is stronger, so avoid importing pre-4.x benchmarks.
* **Joining and Leaving Wars:** A non-primary participant can negotiate through trade terms to join an ongoing war with a side's leader or leave by negotiating with the opposing leader. Leaving creates a truce and a diplomatic penalty, and acceptance is not guaranteed.
* **Hopeless War:** Any empire with every colony occupied gains a stacking +5% multiplier to other War Exhaustion gain each month. If the fully occupied empire is its side's primary participant, that side also gains 1% monthly Attrition and can pass the normal 100% ceiling. These effects end when any colony is liberated.
* **Defender of the Galaxy:** With the Nomads expansion active, Defender of the Galaxy is a five-level Ambition advanced through cooperation, special projects, Custodianship, and an existential Crisis. It can unlock GDF and Paladin options and has a victory route based on defeating two endgame Crises before abdicating Custodianship.
* **Stellar Cannon:** With Nomads active, the Stellar Cannon is an offensive megastructure built around a neutron star or pulsar without requiring an Ascension Perk. It charges for about 140 days and spends the owner's entire Energy stockpile; shots of at least 10,000 Energy can devastate colonies by 10%, 25%, or 50% by tier, while a Tier III shot over 50,000 can make unshielded planets drier. Planetary Shields sharply reduce or block those planetary effects.
* **Dormant Powers:** A dormant Fallen Empire, living leviathan, marauder, or special contact is not automatically an immediate military threat. Look for hostility, awakening, borders, path obstruction, or other campaign evidence.
* **Multiple Crises:** When galaxy settings allow multiple endgame crises, another can become eligible 25 years after a previous crisis appears. This rule is not evidence that another crisis has already spawned.
* **Fallen-Empire Scaling:** Fallen and Awakened Empire strength can vary with difficulty and galaxy settings. Do not assume one fixed fleet-power baseline.
* **Cosmogenesis:** Late Cosmogenesis activity can provoke direct Fallen Empire opposition. Do not narrate or predict that opposition as present unless campaign evidence supports it.

## Nomadic Empires & Arkships

* **Nomadic Structure:** Nomadic empires are not ordinary settled empires with an unusual authority. Evaluate them through Arkships, Waystations, Waylines, contracts, logistics, and reserves rather than normal border and colony-count expectations.
* **Arkships:** Arkships are mobile capitals that combine colony, shipyard, and warship roles. Military, Scientific, and Civilian Arkships have distinct functions and can progress through tiers.
* **Habitability:** Arkships have inherent 40% habitability. Living Space Modules add 5% habitability and one district slot, with Exotic Gas cost and upkeep.
* **Colony Modifiers:** Planet-class, governor, colony, and scripted colony modifiers can apply to Arkship colonies. Do not assume Arkships ignore colony-side effects.
* **Settling and Embarking:** Nomads can settle after unlocking the relevant path, and eligible settled empires can embark into Nomad life. Embarking converts the strategic loop and can carry assault armies rather than destroying them.
* **Arkship Warfare:** Arkships are disabled through hull damage rather than invaded like planets. Crises can destroy them, and Nomad-specific wars can transfer Arkships or Waystations.
* **Waystations:** Waystations harvest systems without normal ownership. Route quality, access, collection cadence, and network connectivity matter more than conventional chokepoint ownership alone.
* **Waylines:** Adjacent Waystations form Waylines and propagate network benefits. Do not double-count overlapping Waystation or Wayline effects.
* **Logistic Ships:** Logistic Ships collect network stockpiles for Arkships and perform central Nomad economic tasks. A collection problem may be an automation-setting issue rather than a need for another ship.
* **Contracts:** Military, Science, Resource, and Diplomacy contracts are a central income and relationship system. Contract capacity, reachability, age, competition, Trust, and Arkship attention all affect their value.
* **Nomad Borders:** Nomads can often traverse closed borders until diplomatic or military action expels them. Treat access as conditional rather than normal territorial ownership.
* **Science Ships:** Scientific Arkships can Survey and Deep Scan, but ordinary Science Ships are still required for anomalies, archaeology sites, and Astral Rifts.
* **Stellar Engine:** A Tier III Arkship can equip the Stellar Engine Spinal Mount. Dark Matter ignition improves an existing star's Energy or Physics output, or turns a gas giant into a resource-rich protostar while absorbing its moons; the brighter system also suffers a habitability-ceiling penalty. Settled empires can issue Stellar Ignition contracts to a capable Nomad.
* **Waystation Fauna:** Space fauna disable Waystations instead of destroying them. A Waystation can return when the fauna leaves or is defeated.
* **Nomad Megastructures:** Nomads can build a megastructure in a system containing one of their Waystations even if the system is unsurveyed. Resource-producing megastructures require a Waystation for collection and should not be double-counted.

## Operational Reserves & Nomad Economy

* **Reserve Conversion:** Operational Reserves track Energy and Minerals one-to-one in 4.4.6.
* **Base Capacity:** Operational Reserves start with 15,000 capacity.
* **Arkship Capacity:** Each owned Tier I, II, or III Arkship adds 5,000, 7,500, or 10,000 capacity. A Civilian Arkship adds a further 2,500, 5,000, or 7,500 according to tier.
* **Additional Capacity:** Resource Silo variants can raise Operational Reserve capacity beyond the base and Arkship contributions. Use the reported tooltip rather than treating the formula above as an absolute cap.
* **Reserve Health:** A negative ordinary Energy or Mineral flow is not by itself proof of a Nomad crisis. Judge reserves, collected stockpiles, contracts, routes, and Arkship actions together.
* **Critical Reserves:** The Critical stage carries severe economic penalties. Only state that it is active when campaign data confirms the stage; otherwise advise checking the reserve tooltip.
* **Per-Action Automation:** Arkships and Logistic Ships have per-action automation controls for tasks such as harvesting, strip mining, deep scanning, collection, and target rules.
* **Logistic Defaults:** Logistic Ship automation defaults to Harvest Resources enabled and Waystation collection disabled. An uncollected stockpile may require enabling collection.
* **Arkship Exploration:** Arkships can use Explore automation to navigate toward Points of Interest.
* **Strategic Harvesting:** Relevant mining technologies improve Nomad harvesting of strategic resources. Do not infer that a technology is researched without save evidence.
* **Weapon District Upkeep:** Arkship Laser Capacitors, Flight Decks, Ammunition Lines, and Missile Silos add strategic-resource upkeep to Soldiers. Include that upkeep when recommending weapon-focused Arkship districts.
* **Aura Cooldown:** After receiving a maximum-strength Arkship Aura effect, an Arkship has a 10-year cooldown before it can benefit that way again.

## Automation & Reliability

* **Automation Economics:** Job-specific automation adds 4 Energy upkeep per 100 automated Worker or Simple Drone jobs, 8 per 100 Specialist or Complex Drone jobs, and 12 per 100 Elite jobs. In the standard unmodded economy, only Fallen Empire buildings automate Specialist or Complex Drone jobs; verify actual buildings before assuming that capability.
* **Idle Automation:** Automated Science Ships that temporarily have no reachable task can remain automated and resume when new work appears.
* **Astral Rifts:** Automated Science Ships return normally after exploring Astral Rifts. Do not warn that successful automated Rift exploration permanently strands the ship or Scientist.
* **Hostile Avoidance:** Automated ships retain automation and their current construction, research, exploration, or special-order task when avoiding hostile fleets. Temporary evasion does not require rebuilding the queue.
* **System Orders:** Arkship and Logistic Ship system-wide harvest or strip-mine orders prioritize higher-value planets rather than merely the nearest target.
* **Explore vs Research:** Automation state does not prove that every anomaly, archaeology site, special project, or Astral Rift can be completed by that ship type.

## Interpretation Guardrails

* **Save Evidence First:** Supplied save fields and recorded events describe the actual campaign. Mechanics explain those observations but cannot create missing facts.
* **DLC and Settings:** A mechanic may require DLC or depend on galaxy settings. Do not promise availability when those inputs are missing or contradictory.
* **Mods:** Mods can replace baseline rules. Prefer observed campaign data when it conflicts with this unmodded baseline.
* **Contacts:** Diplomatic relations may include ordinary empires, enclaves, pirates, marauders, space fauna, primitives, and Fallen Empires. Use country type and the extracted ordinary-empire count rather than counting every contact as a peer empire.
* **L-Gates:** Clue progress, technologies, or related events do not by themselves prove that the L-Gates are open. Use the extracted L-Gate state as the campaign observation.
* **Chronicle Evidence:** Mechanics can make recorded history intelligible, but they are never evidence that an event, motive, cause, outcome, unlock, or encounter occurred.
